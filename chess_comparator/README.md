# Nibbler Chess · dual analysis prototype

Version 14 uses the full supplied Lucas Chess R2 resource archive for the integrated library: all 51 complete piece sets, 41 Polyglot `.bin` books, all Gaviota table files, and the FNS collections in `Trainings` and `Tactics`. The resources tab lets you switch the board style, inspect book move weights, probe Gaviota endgames through five pieces, and choose one training collection or all collections for randomized positions. FNS exercises with a recorded solution accept the principal line, with the opponent's replies played automatically; position-only endgames use the selected chess engine to set a target move. There is also a separate best-move challenge. Solved exercises count in the local profile. The full 110 MB resource subset is under `assets/lucas_resources`; pre-rendered piece images work offline on Windows. Upstream license and individual piece-set attribution/license files are included. The entire Lucas Chess application is not embedded or started by this project.

Version 13 adds **Resources and exercises / Recursos y ejercicios**. Choose Nibbler, Celtic, Fantasy or Eyes pieces; the three additional styles come from Lucas Chess R2, by Maurizio Monge, and include their original SVG files, attribution and LGPL 2.1 license text next to the converted PNGs. The included `GMopenings.bin` Polyglot opening book shows weighted move suggestions, and another `.bin` can be chosen at runtime. Five small Gaviota tables support three-piece endgame probes; choose a directory with more `.gtb.cp4` files for broader coverage (up to five pieces). The tab also has 60 one-move mate puzzles derived from Lucas Chess R2's Eduardo Sadier training set and a current-position best-move challenge scored by the chosen engine. Solved exercises are counted in the local training profile. The book and endgame probes are reference tools, not an opening-line trainer or full endgame course. Lucas Chess R2 is licensed GPL-3.0; `LUCAS-CHESS-LICENSE.txt` is included. The artwork's additional license files are kept in `assets/lucas_styles/<style>/`. The data files and puzzles are attributed to Lucas Chess R2's `Resources` at revision `b3ab7678077d6433e45d85f55acac2102e13e82e`.

Version 12 adds an English/Español selector above the board, highlights the from/to squares of the most recent move, and adds **Takeback**, **Offer draw**, and **Resign** beneath the game controls. Takeback cancels an engine search and returns to the human turn, normally removing the engine and human moves together. A draw offer receives a simulated engine decision based on a brief position evaluation; it is not a negotiation feature supplied by UCI/XBoard. If accepted or resigned, the result is stored in a saved game PGN. Resigning shows a friendly invitation to press **Analyze**. English covers the main controls, tutor feedback and tactical panel; existing PGN chess notation and engine names remain language independent. The selected language lasts for the current run.

Version 11 adds local training profiles. On startup choose an existing profile, create one with your name, prior chess experience and optional self-reported Elo, or continue as a guest. **Perfil / progreso** opens the same window later. In engine games, each human move is counted and an available engine scores it even if the automatic tutor display is off. The profile stores counts by opening, middlegame and endgame, rough move-quality categories, hints and the last 100 evaluated moves. After at least five evaluated moves, the plan prioritizes a phase only if it has at least three evaluated moves and at least one estimated mistake. Profiles are saved in your Windows user folder under `NibblerDualAnalysis/profiles.json`; nothing is sent online. The entered Elo remains self-reported and is not adjusted by the application. Engine judgments depend on short searches and are approximate. Do not interpret these counts as an official rating or comprehensive assessment.

Version 10 adds the **Tutor y personajes** tab beside **Análisis**. Einstein offers optional engine-based hints, position explanations, and automatic feedback after your moves. The selected rival appears as a dinosaur (Crafty) or blue piranha (Stockfish), changes expression according to its estimated advantage, and offers brief phase-specific tips and playful messages. Activate **Tutor automático** in the tab for feedback; **Pista** and **Explicar** work on demand. The explanations are short heuristics based on the principal variation and a limited engine search, so they cannot establish the full tactical reason for every move. The user-provided character drawings and generated happy/worried variants are bundled in `assets`.

A personal-use desktop analysis prototype with the board piece artwork from Nibbler. Stockfish and Crafty run as independent processes; their first choices and variations appear side by side. Enter a FEN, load a PGN (the last position), or click squares to play, then press **Analyze**. Stockfish shows three candidate lines; Crafty shows one. A positive evaluation favors the player to move. “Mate in N” is the engine's score and depends on search time; it is not a proof for arbitrary longer mates.

The **Partida → Personalidades / Game → Personalities** dialog follows the selected English/Español language. Its labels, buttons, champion titles, countries, and playing-style descriptions are translated for all 18 grandmasters; names, Elo values, and algebraic opening statistics remain unchanged.

## PDF chess-book reader

Choose **File → Open chess book PDF** (or press **Ctrl+Shift+B**) to read a book beside the analysis board. The reader shows rendered pages, extracted/OCR text, page navigation, and zoom. In **Follow moves**, paste one move sequence and choose Spanish descriptive notation or algebraic SAN. For descriptive notation, first set up the position shown in the book's diagram with **Edit → Edit position**. RoboChess validates the entire line against legal moves, displays the converted SAN line, and plays it on the board. Choose an engine and press **Analyze board** to inspect the resulting position; the line can also be saved as PGN.

To study a printed diagram, open its PDF page and choose **Select diagram with mouse**. Drag around the board. RoboChess opens the selected image as a reference; choose **Set up position**, place the pieces with the main-board editor, choose whose turn it is, and press **Apply**. RoboChess then loads the position and starts analysis with the selected engine. Piece recognition is manual in this version, so compare the board with the reference before applying it. Castling rights and en-passant state are not visible in a static diagram; enter those through FEN when they matter.

Descriptive notation depends on the exact position and can be ambiguous. The reader reports a move it cannot resolve instead of guessing. The attached *Fundamentos del ajedrez* PDF is a ClearScan/OCR scan; OCR sometimes confuses characters in move text, so check the converted line against the printed page. The reader does not rewrite every page of the source PDF. Install its optional renderer from `requirements-pdf.txt` with `py -m pip install -r requirements-pdf.txt`.

## Start

Python 3.10+ with Tkinter is needed. The `python-chess` package is included in `vendor` for offline use. On Linux run `python3 app.py`; on Windows run `py app.py`. Choose the Stockfish 19 executable and Crafty executable using the two buttons. The supplied Linux Crafty binary is preselected on Linux. The uploaded Stockfish archive includes a Windows `.exe`, which you can select after extracting it. The uploaded Crafty source does not include a Windows executable: compile its `src/makefile.win` with the required compiler, or obtain a compatible Crafty build from its author, then select it in the app. Nibbler's original Electron source is separate: this is a new interface using its board artwork, not a patch to Nibbler's single-engine analysis loop.

The tactical panel flags observable checks, captures, pins and fork candidates from the engine's first move. A possible sacrifice means a moved piece is attacked; it is not verified as sound. Longer combinations and punishment of mistakes come from engine search and must be checked by comparing the lines after the opponent's move. This version has no automatic exhaustive mate-in-N solver or certified blunder classification.

## Export for ChessBase

Analyze a position, then click **Save analysis PGN**. The file contains the played moves and each available principal variation as a PGN variation; the first move of each line has an engine name, depth and standard `[%eval ...]` annotation from White’s perspective. ChessBase can import this ordinary `.pgn` file. **Save analysis TXT** writes the position FEN and readable engine lines. The export reflects the last completed analysis; run Analyze again after changing the board. Nibbler itself already has **File → Save this game…** for game PGN; that native option and this comparison export are different.

## Crafty as a UCI engine

`crafty_uci.py` translates UCI commands to the original Crafty XBoard protocol using python-chess. Crafty's search code is untouched. On Windows compile Crafty and place `crafty.exe` beside the script, then choose `Crafty-UCI.bat` in a UCI-compatible GUI (or set the `CRAFTY_PATH` environment variable to your Crafty executable). The batch file needs Python 3 installed. On Linux run `python3 crafty_uci.py` and configure that command as the engine. The launcher supports `position`, `go depth`, `go nodes`, `go movetime`, `go infinite`, `stop`, and standard status commands. It reports only one principal variation and may exceed the requested time because Crafty's analysis timing is approximate. This is a bridge, not a rebuilt Crafty engine.

This package omits Crafty source trees, opening books, test data, and build files; its supplied Linux Crafty binary is retained for Linux use. Keep your original Crafty archive if you need to compile Windows Crafty or inspect its source.

## Lichess Video Library (optional)

Open **View → Lichess Video Library** (or press **Ctrl+Shift+L**) to show the official video library in the right-hand panel. Use **Close videos · Back to Study / Cerrar videos · Volver al estudio** to return to RoboChess; reopen the library from View at any time. Closing the module stops playback and discards its temporary WebView. The analysis board stays on the left for studying video positions. Video moves are not synchronized to the board automatically because the official library does not expose a general lesson PGN feed.

The WebView permits only HTTPS video-library routes and the lesson's privacy-enhanced YouTube embed. Lichess login/game pages, popups, and external-browser handoff are blocked; the view runs with a temporary session. On Windows, install the WebView2 Runtime if it is missing, then run `py -m pip install -r requirements-video.txt --upgrade` and restart RoboChess. If the embedded runtime cannot start, the module shows an error instead of opening the full Lichess site externally.

## DGT board reset

When the physical DGT board is returned to the standard starting setup, RoboChess now resets the current game automatically, stops the active engine and clock, and restores White to move. Intermediate piece lifts and placements are ignored. The automatic reset is disabled in editor, puzzle, review, engine-match, and quad-tournament modes.

## If Crafty shows a ping error

Version 4 automatically recognizes `Crafty-UCI.bat` as UCI and a native Crafty executable as XBoard. Select the correct file using **Crafty…**. If you select `Crafty-UCI.bat`, put a compiled Windows `crafty.exe` in the same folder as the launcher. The original Crafty source ZIP alone is not an executable. Stockfish must be selected separately under **Stockfish…**.

## Version 5 Windows build

`crafty.exe` was cross-compiled from your supplied Crafty source with MinGW-w64 for Windows x86-64, using `-O2 -D WINDOWS -DCPUS=1 -DNO_INTRIN`. It needs only standard Windows `KERNEL32.dll` and `msvcrt.dll` imports. This build avoids newer CPU instruction requirements and uses one CPU thread. The executable is preselected in the program; press **Analyze** after choosing your Stockfish executable. `Crafty-UCI.bat` remains available if another UCI GUI needs Crafty. Windows runtime execution was not available in the build environment, so if Windows reports an error, send the exact message and the output from `cmd`. The supplied Linux Crafty binary was independently tested with the analysis protocol.

## Version 6 board display

The board uses cream and green squares like the reference image. Once analysis finishes, Stockfish candidate moves 1–3 appear as green, blue, and gold arrows labeled S1–S3; Crafty's first move is an orange arrow labeled C. Use the checkboxes above the board to hide either engine’s arrows. The bar on the left has Black at the top and White at the bottom. It uses Stockfish’s white-perspective evaluation if available, otherwise Crafty’s. The orange horizontal marker shows Crafty’s own evaluation when both engines have results. A white bar that occupies more than half indicates a White advantage; an all-dark bar indicates a Black advantage. Mate scores push the bar near the appropriate end. Saved PGN includes all three Stockfish candidate lines and Crafty's principal line.

## Version 7 Crafty startup fix

The user’s Windows console showed `feature done=1` before the XBoard `protover 2` request. Version 7 includes a patched Windows `crafty.exe` and passes the `xboard` startup argument. The small source change and build command are in `CRAFTY-XBOARD-PATCH.diff` and `BUILD-CRAFTY.md`.

## Version 8 Crafty work files

Crafty still needs its own `game.NNN` history file while it runs. The program starts it in a private temporary directory, forces `CRAFTY_LOG_PATH` to that directory, and removes the directory after the engine exits. The optional UCI launcher uses the same approach. New `log.NNN` and `game.NNN` files will no longer accumulate beside `app.py`. Files created by older versions are left intact; you may manually delete their `log.NNN` and `game.NNN` files when the program is closed.

## Version 9: play and flip

Click **Girar tablero** to rotate the board 180°. Coordinates, piece clicks, engine arrows, and the evaluation bar follow the chosen view. To play, select **Jugar contra** Stockfish or Crafty, select **Tu color**, enter **Nivel 1–3800** and **s/jugada**, then click **Nueva partida**. If you choose Black, the board rotates and the engine makes the first move. Click a piece and its destination to move; queens are chosen automatically on promotion. **Terminar** leaves the final board available for inspection; **Guardar PGN** saves the played game. **Analyze** leaves play mode and runs comparison analysis.

The level input is an approximate difficulty control, **not a certified Elo rating**. Stockfish 19 accepts UCI_Elo 1320–3190. Crafty's internal Elo setting (enabled in this new `crafty.exe` build by `-DELO`) accepts about 800–3599; 3600 or more disables the limit. Below the engine's minimum the game occasionally picks a random legal move as a handicap. Above its range it plays at full configured strength for the selected thinking time. Thus 3800 means full strength, not a measured 3800 Elo. The board also has no promotion chooser yet; promotions become queens.
